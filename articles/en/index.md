Essays on enterprise AI deployment, AI-native R&D, and how to manage a team of AI agents. Each one is the English edition of a Chinese original.

## [Why your agents won't work, and why you should stop building your company's own agent](./why-your-agents-wont-work.html)

You've rolled out agents and added skills, yet you keep hiring and the results still don't show. The problem usually isn't too few agents; it's that your company's knowledge is locked inside each one. Here are the seven findings from two years of doing this, and three things to do when you get back.

## [Product managers still writing PRDs are adorable](./still-writing-prd.html)

You turn requirements into a document and have AI follow it item by item, yet features still go missing or land in the wrong place. The problem isn't that the document lacks detail; it's that you never needed to write it. It was only ever a translation between business and engineering. Here's how to work without it.

## [Rolling out AI to a team? Why you should start with fewer Agents and Skills](./why-teams-should-start-with-fewer-agents-and-skills.html)

You assign an Agent to every role and keep adding Skills, yet people can't tell which one to call, and work that spans several people still has to be pulled together by hand. The problem isn't that there are too few; it's that the entry points, the information and acceptance were never connected. Here's how to work out a setup that's just enough, starting from one task.

## [The scarcest people in R&D now write the rules, not the code](./ai-rd-rule-makers.html)

You gave your team AI coding tools, and code really did get faster, yet rework and production issues haven't gone down. The problem usually isn't that AI isn't strong enough; it's that the process was still designed for people writing code. This essay covers how to set rules for AI, and how to spend the first 30 days.

## [You're using Agents more and more, so why is the good version getting harder to find?](./agent-collaboration-blind-spot.html)

You have the Agent make change after change and click Allow at every step, yet in the end you can't say which version is the right one. The problem usually isn't that you don't understand the tech; it's that no one ever taught you how to work with it. Here are five things you should say to it.

## [The next trillion-dollar company sells the work, not the tool](./services-the-new-software.html)

The people around you who use AI keep getting better, yet they sit far from the business; the people who own the business can't keep up with the tools. What's broken isn't the tools. It's that nobody in the middle is responsible for connecting them to the business. This piece is about who should pick up that layer.

## [What does an FDE actually do all day? I took apart 34 real job descriptions from Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou and Shenzhen](./what-fde-does-34-job-descriptions.html)

You might think an FDE just deploys models, but 34 real job descriptions show that 94% start by finding the business problem and 91% also build things themselves. Why does this role look like a mix of presales, product, engineering and implementation? This piece is about what companies really want this person to own.

## [The next bottleneck in AI R&D isn't building the work. It's accepting it.](./ai-rd-next-bottleneck-acceptance.html)

AI writes code faster and faster, so why is R&D still not fast? The real bottleneck is moving from executing tasks to understanding requirements, delivery evidence and human acceptance. This essay uses five questions and answers to explain multiple Agents, PRDs, product meetings, repository maps and how acceptance works.

## [The harder you study AI, the longer you stay a super-worker](./stop-learning-ai-start-managing-it.html)

You bought the courses, saved hundreds of prompts and cycled through a round of tools, and you still haven't got your time back. AI stopped lacking capability a while ago; what's missing is whether you dare hand one job over completely. This piece covers how to hand over your first job in week one, and what counts as done.

## [What enterprise AI lacks isn't tools. It's someone who forces the results.](./enterprise-ai-needs-caio-and-fde.html)

You bought the models, ran the training, even watched the demos, and the business hasn't changed at all. The problem usually isn't the tools; it's that nobody ties goals, execution and acceptance together. This piece explains what that person actually does, and how the first 30 days go.

## [Your R&D team keeps growing. Why is the product still slow?](./ai-native-rd-team-optimization.html)

You keep hiring, and projects still slip. Meanwhile, at some companies AI already generates 99.9% of the code. The difference isn't headcount; it's that you still define R&D as writing code. This essay covers how an R&D team makes the AI-native transformation, and how to spend the first 30 days.
